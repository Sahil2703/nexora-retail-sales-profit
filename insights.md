# Business Insights — Nexora Retail

## 1. Overview

This analysis uses **Nexora Retail's sales** dataset, covering retail transactions from 2014 through 2017. The objective is to understand where sales and profits come from, identify areas of weak profitability, and highlight opportunities for improvement.

The business generated **$2.30 million in sales and $286,397 in profit**, with an overall profit margin of **12.47%**. However, these overall figures conceal substantial differences between product categories, regions, and individual transactions.

| Key Metric | Result |
|---|---:|
| Total Sales | $2,297,200.86 |
| Total Profit | $286,397.02 |
| Distinct Orders | 5,009 |
| Profit Margin | 12.47% |
| Average Order Value | $458.61 |
| Records with Negative Profit | 18.72% |

Average order value is calculated as total sales divided by distinct orders. The dataset contains 9,994 order-line records and 5,009 distinct orders; these counts represent different things.

## 2. Sales Growth and Profitability

Annual sales declined slightly in 2015 before increasing in the following two years.

| Year | Sales | Profit |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2014 | $484,247 | $49,544 |
| 2015 | $470,533 | $61,619 |
| 2016 | $609,206 | $81,795 |
| 2017 | $733,215 | $93,439 |

Sales increased by approximately **51.4% between 2014 and 2017**. Profit also increased over the period, although the annual totals alone do not show whether margins improved consistently.

The monthly trend varies considerably, with some periods contributing more sales and profit than others. Looking at monthly results alongside annual totals helps identify periods when sales and profitability move differently.

**Business implication:** Monitor monthly sales, profit, and margin together. Sales growth is valuable, but it should not be treated as the only measure of business performance.

## 3. Regional Performance

The West generated the highest sales and profit among the four regions. Central, by comparison, had the lowest regional profit margin.

| Region | Sales | Profit | Profit Margin |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| West | $725,458 | $108,418 | 14.94% |
| East | $678,781 | $91,523 | 13.48% |
| South | $391,722 | $46,749 | 11.93% |
| Central | $501,240 | $39,706 | 7.92% |

The West contributed approximately 37.9% of total profit. Central's margin of 7.92% was considerably below the overall margin of 12.47%.

A closer look at Central shows further differences between categories. Furniture recorded a loss of approximately $2,871, while Office Supplies generated a margin of around 5.32%. Technology performed considerably better, with a margin of approximately 19.77%.

**Business implication:** Investigate Central's product mix, pricing, and discounting, particularly in Furniture and Office Supplies. The West provides a useful point of comparison, but its performance should not be assumed to be directly transferable to other regions.

## 4. Category Profitability

Technology and Office Supplies generated most of the business's profit. Furniture contributed substantial sales but delivered a much smaller return.

| Category | Sales | Profit | Profit Margin |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Technology | $836,154 | $145,455 | 17.40% |
| Office Supplies | $719,047 | $122,491 | 17.04% |
| Furniture | $742,000 | $18,451 | 2.49% |

Technology accounted for approximately 50.8% of total profit, while Office Supplies contributed another 42.8%. Furniture represented roughly 32.3% of sales but only 6.4% of profit.

This is the clearest category-level profitability gap in the analysis. Furniture is not unprofitable as a whole, but its overall return is much lower than that of the other categories.

**Business implication:** Review Furniture pricing, discount levels, and product-level margins. The goal should be to improve profitability without unnecessarily sacrificing sales volume.

## 5. Sub-Category and Product Performance

Sub-category results show where profit is being generated and where losses are concentrated.

**Strong profit contributors**

- **Copiers:** approximately $55,618 in profit.
- **Phones:** approximately $44,516 in profit.
- **Accessories:** approximately $41,937 in profit.
- **Paper:** approximately $34,054 in profit.
- **Binders:** approximately $30,222 in profit.

**Loss-making sub-categories**

- **Tables:** approximately $64,083 in losses.
- **Bookcases:** approximately $3,473 in losses.
- **Supplies:** approximately $1,189 in losses.

Tables stands out as the largest loss-making sub-category. Its losses offset a meaningful portion of the profit generated elsewhere. Bookcases and Supplies also recorded losses, although on a much smaller scale.

Product-level results add another layer to the analysis. The Canon imageCLASS 2200 Advanced Copier generated approximately $25,200 in profit on $61,600 in sales, making it a strong contributor. Other products generated substantial sales but still finished with negative profit.

**Business implication:** Review Tables first, followed by Bookcases and Supplies. At the product level, examine selling prices, discounts, and margins before deciding whether to adjust pricing, revise promotions, or reconsider particular products.

## 6. Discount Impact

Discounting is another area that deserves attention.

| Discount Group | Profit |
|---|---:|
| Discounted Orders | -$34,590.58 |
| Full-Price Orders | $320,987.60 |

Approximately 52.6% of sales revenue came from discounted orders. In aggregate, the discounted-order group generated a loss, while the full-price group generated substantial profit.

This relationship is worth investigating, but it does not prove that discounts alone caused the losses. The groups may differ in product mix, order size, customer segment, and other factors that affect profitability.

**Business implication:** Compare profit and margin across discount levels, particularly for Tables and other weak-performing products. Where possible, compare similar products and orders before changing discount policies.

## 7. Recommended Actions

Based on the analysis, five actions deserve priority:

1. **Investigate Tables profitability.** Review discounts, pricing, and product-level results to understand the source of the substantial losses.
2. **Examine Central's category mix.** Start with Furniture and Office Supplies, where margins are weakest.
3. **Review discount effectiveness.** Determine whether additional sales from promotions compensate for the profit sacrificed.
4. **Protect profitable categories and products.** Monitor Technology, Office Supplies, Copiers, Phones, and Accessories to understand what contributes to their strong results.
5. **Track sales and profit together.** Use monthly reporting to distinguish genuine business improvement from growth accompanied by weaker profitability.

These actions should be tested against transaction-level results before major pricing or product-assortment decisions are made.

## 8. Conclusion

Nexora Retail's results show a business with substantial sales and positive overall profit, but profitability is uneven. Technology and Office Supplies contribute most of the profit, the West is the strongest region, and Central has a clear profitability gap. Furniture—and particularly Tables—deserves the closest review.

The main takeaway is straightforward: **growing sales is valuable, but understanding which sales generate sustainable profit is just as important.**
